# GlucoTwin explanation and demo script

Synthetic narration is disclosed in the generated video. Doctor review remains pending.

## 1. GlucoTwin

GlucoTwin is a research prototype for a diabetes clinic and its care team. Its purpose is to connect an evolving patient model to a review process that can be inspected. A forecast by itself is only a number. The clinician needs to know which observations were available, how recent they are, how uncertain the prediction is, and what happened after the prediction was issued. The product brings these pieces together. This walkthrough explains the use case, shows the implemented application, describes the model and evaluation, and makes the remaining clinical gates explicit. The data are public and anonymized, or clearly synthetic in integration scenarios. Recorded patient playback is identified as recorded playback. This presentation uses synthetic narration and an automated recording of the local prototype. Doctor feedback and prospective clinical validation are pending. The intended outcome of this build is a reproducible and reviewable foundation for a supervised pilot.

## 2. Clinic review workflow

The core journey starts with the clinic review queue. A care-team member chooses a patient who has an unresolved forecast review item or a data-quality concern. The patient view shows glucose history together with historical context and the issued forecast. The reviewer can inspect probability, intervals, data freshness and adaptation maturity before recording a decision. The action record contains the reviewer, time, reason, note and next review date when appropriate. As playback advances, an observed outcome becomes available and can be compared with the earlier forecast. That comparison belongs to the evidence, whether the prediction succeeded or failed. The workflow is deliberately narrow. It supports accountable review of a defined glucose excursion rather than claiming to manage every aspect of diabetes. Meals, activity and symptoms can be recorded as context. They do not become validated predictive signals just because a form exists. This keeps the product's useful workflow separate from claims that still need evidence.

## 3. Challenge alignment

The challenge asks for a focused chronic-condition prototype that combines historical patient context with a dynamic sensor stream, predicts a localized adverse outcome, and shows how a doctor interacts with it. GlucoTwin addresses those requirements with Type 2 diabetes, matched historical context, native continuous-glucose-monitoring observations and a future high-glucose-excursion label. Public or synthetic data are accepted by the brief, so an approved live-device connection is not a dependency for the demonstration. The thirty-minute target is our primary evaluation horizon, with sixty minutes reported separately. The brief's longer glucose example is illustrative. Our demonstration makes the evidence chain visible: inputs, patient state, forecast, review and later outcome. This is also the basis of our practical differentiation. The published page does not provide numerical judging weights, and we do not invent them. The submission materials describe actual implementation and measured outcomes rather than estimating a probability of winning.

## 4. Measured CGM at native resolution

The primary source is the Shanghai Type 2 diabetes dataset. Our inspected and prepared archive contains one hundred patient base identifiers and one hundred and nine recordings. There are one hundred and twelve thousand, four hundred and sixty-two usable measured glucose observations. The native interval is fifteen minutes. Thirteen rows with missing required values were excluded, and eleven native gaps were retained. We do not create additional measurements by interpolating between sensor observations. All recordings belonging to a patient remain in the same evaluation partition. The interface uses one primary recording for the recorded demonstration, while evaluation can include every eligible recording. The dataset has matched clinical summaries, and its original attribution remains separate from the software license. These counts establish what was loaded and audited. They do not establish clinical accuracy, Indian generalization or the suitability of a future outpatient deployment. The data manifest and preparation script make the audit reproducible.

## 5. Historical context and availability

Historical information needs a time boundary as carefully as sensor information. A laboratory result measured earlier but received later was not necessarily available to the model when a forecast was issued. New intake therefore distinguishes observation time from arrival or availability time. Patient identity and provenance are also retained. The original Shanghai summary provides age, sex, body mass index and diabetes duration with documented availability assumptions. Untimed laboratory values and medication information are excluded from prediction features. This matters because the source study permits some laboratory collection after the glucose monitoring period. A synthetic EHR scenario can demonstrate dated diagnoses, observations and care plans without claiming that an unrelated record belongs to a measured participant. The two types of evidence stay separate. Patient-entered meal, activity and symptom entries are labelled self-reported context. They appear in the timeline but do not silently become inputs to a validated treatment or meal-response model.

## 6. System architecture

The system keeps a short and readable architecture. React and TypeScript provide the clinic interface. FastAPI handles intake, patient evidence, monitoring configuration and review actions. The forecasting module consumes an observation prefix and the historical context available at that point. SQLite stores observations, context, issued forecasts, review episodes and action records. Schema versions support repeatable migrations. The existing recorded replay remains useful for controlled demonstrations. The upgraded model adds population and adapted forecasts, event probabilities and a maturity record. A forecast record keeps its issue time, input cutoff and model version so later data cannot rewrite what was known earlier. The review workflow consumes that record and stores human actions separately. Consultation summaries use the same evidence records. This separation is important: acknowledging a warning changes its workflow status, not the model's output or its accuracy. Future outcomes are revealed only after their observation time has been reached.

## 7. Population forecasting foundation

The population forecaster is the starting point for reliable prediction. It uses a gradient-boosted model over causal features extracted from recent native glucose observations. These include current and lagged readings, changes over time, recent mean and variability, time of day and available historical attributes. At least five consecutive native readings are required to construct the expected history. The model is compared with persistence, a simple trend forecast and a context-free version. These baselines answer different questions. Persistence asks whether simply carrying the current value forward is competitive. The trend model asks whether recent movement is sufficient. The context-free comparison asks whether the historical attributes actually improve performance. In the earlier split, the population model was stronger than the original personalized filter, and removing context improved the score. That finding is retained. The existence of an EHR input does not prove that it improves forecasting. The updated evaluation measures that contribution explicitly.

## 8. Online patient adaptation

Patient adaptation is evaluated as an additional contribution, rather than assumed to help. The upgraded candidate applies a bounded and shrunk correction to the population forecast. A correction can update only when a previously issued forecast has reached its target and the corresponding measured outcome is available. It cannot learn from an outcome that remains in the future. Early in a recording there is little patient-specific evidence, so the correction stays close to the population foundation. As outcomes mature, the interface shows adaptation samples and correction history. The engineering state can also describe recent level, trend and variability. These quantities do not establish a mechanistic simulation of metabolism or insulin. The appropriate comparison is the identical population engine with adaptation disabled. We claim superior personalization only if the paired patient-bootstrap uncertainty interval supports it. When that gate fails, the primary forecast remains the population model and the adapted candidate is labelled experimental.

## 9. Future-excursion prediction

A glucose value at the end of a horizon is not the same as an excursion anywhere inside the horizon. The earlier alert rule used an endpoint forecast above one hundred and eighty milligrams per deciliter. That can miss a transient excursion that rises and falls before the endpoint. The upgraded pipeline therefore trains a dedicated classifier whose label is any measured glucose above the research threshold within the next thirty minutes. Sixty-minute classification is evaluated separately. Windows with unobservable targets or unsupported history are handled by the frozen protocol rather than treated as confirmed negatives. An ongoing high reading is identified separately from a prediction of a new excursion. The displayed probability is calibrated using patients separate from those used to fit the model. An alert episode is deduplicated across repeated readings. The event label is a research definition, and it does not by itself establish that a particular clinical intervention is appropriate.

## 10. Calibration and alert burden

Forecast reliability has several parts. Predictive intervals describe uncertainty around the future glucose value. Event calibration asks whether displayed probabilities correspond to observed frequencies. Both need evaluation on data separate from model fitting. The warning threshold is selected on calibration or validation data using a provisional false-alert budget. The initial engineering target is one false-alert episode per twenty-four eligible monitored hours. This is a prototype operating point, not a clinical standard. The benchmark reports false alarms, missed episodes, warning lead time and the precision-recall tradeoff. The denominator is identified so that below-threshold eligible time is not confused with all calendar time. Data-quality abstention is also reported. A forecast paused during a gap should not look like a low-risk prediction. Fixed residual-based intervals are empirical estimates, and their aggregate coverage does not guarantee coverage for every patient. Clinical acceptance criteria and response expectations remain subject to the partner doctor's review.

## 11. Patient-separated internal validation

The upgraded evaluation uses five outer patient folds. Every recording belonging to a patient stays together. Inside each outer training partition, grouped inner validation selects from predeclared model parameters, and separate patients calibrate probability and intervals. The resulting evaluation predictions are out of fold. Each patient is scored by a model that did not fit that patient's outcomes. We report every fold, pooled results and bootstrap uncertainty resampled by patient. This avoids treating overlapping forecast windows as independent patients. The original seventy-fifteen-fifteen split remains as a legacy benchmark. Its evaluation patients and failure cases were already inspected, so we do not rename a new subset untouched validation. The new report is stronger internal retrospective evidence. Independent confirmation still requires a suitable external cohort or a prospective study. Cold-start and adapted periods are shown separately, and failures remain part of the report rather than being removed from the demonstration narrative.

## 12. Measured model evidence

The application's benchmark view presents measured results and their interpretation. The legacy thirty-minute mean absolute errors were twelve point seven six for persistence, ten point five three for the population forecaster, and fourteen point four eight for the original personalized filter. The original filter therefore was not our best predictor. The completed five-fold benchmark reports nine point eight one two for the population model and nine point six zero six for the hybrid. The paired difference is minus zero point two zero six, with a ninety-five percent patient-bootstrap interval from minus zero point three three six to minus zero point one one zero. This supports a small internal improvement. Thirty-minute episode precision is sixty-two point nine six percent and overall observed episode recall is seventy-six point zero six percent. False alerts average zero point nine one nine per twenty-four eligible forecast hours. This denominator includes all eligible time, not just staffed hours. Historical context benefit was inconclusive. The primary model is chosen according to the predeclared evidence gate, with the adapted candidate kept experimental when superiority is not supported. These are retrospective errors in milligrams per deciliter. Event precision, recall, false-alert burden and calibration answer additional questions that mean absolute error alone cannot answer. A low average error can coexist with missed excursions. The benchmark includes unsuccessful predictions and limitations so the reviewer can assess the practical boundary. None of these retrospective scores establishes a benefit from treatment changes or proves clinical effectiveness in Indian clinics.

## 13. Patient review queue

The review queue is the entry point for the care team. It brings the latest known state, forecast review items, reading freshness and responsibility into one place. A forecast concern is distinguishable from a missing-data concern. This prevents stale data from appearing reassuring. Opening a patient brings the reviewer to the evidence behind the item instead of a generic dashboard. A review item refers to issued evidence, and repeated readings do not create an endless stream of duplicate work. The queue also shows review status and overdue handling where configured. Its role is prioritization for human inspection. It is not a claim that the application can make an independent clinical decision. The chosen demonstration follows one actual recorded episode from this entry point through review and observed outcome. That keeps the three-minute story understandable while the benchmark provides the broader evidence needed to judge the model's performance.

## 14. Staffed monitoring windows

A thirty-minute warning has practical meaning only if the workflow accounts for who can review it. The application therefore includes monitored hours and an assigned reviewer. During a staffed window, a review item can be shown to the responsible care team. An overdue indicator identifies an unresolved operational task. Outside the configured window, the interface states that active monitoring is unavailable and retains evidence for later review. It does not imply that someone is watching twenty-four hours a day. Recorded playback uses its simulation clock, while future live intake needs verified observation and arrival times. The doctor review packet asks for response expectations and escalation ownership. These choices should be settled with the clinical partner before any live-care use. The implementation can demonstrate the configuration and review loop now, but it cannot manufacture staffing or guarantee a response. The monitoring status is part of the evidence a reviewer sees alongside probability and freshness.

## 15. Persistent review and follow-up

The review process stores more than a dismissed notification. A new item can be reviewed, followed up and closed through documented actions. Each action records who performed it, when it happened and the reason or note. A next review date can be retained when follow-up is planned. These records survive a browser or application restart. The audit history helps explain what the care team did with the information. It remains separate from prediction generation. Acknowledgement does not change the forecast value, interval, event probability or eventual outcome. This distinction is tested because it is easy for a demonstration to accidentally conflate a completed workflow with a successful prediction. A false alarm can be closed with an explanatory note and remain a false alarm in evaluation. A missed event remains a missed event even if later follow-up was appropriate. The product preserves both kinds of evidence for review.

## 16. Patient evidence timeline

The patient timeline makes the order of information understandable. It combines measured glucose observations, historical context, saved predictions, later outcomes and care-team notes. Each source keeps its provenance. A synthetic diagnosis is marked synthetic. A meal or symptom entered by a person is marked self-reported. If a historical observation arrives late, its availability time is retained instead of making it appear available earlier. Previously issued forecasts preserve their input cutoff. This is essential for an honest reconstruction of a decision. The clinician can see not only what is now known, but what was known when the warning was issued. The timeline also shows data gaps and recent forecast errors. Those details help a reviewer understand why a prediction may be uncertain or unsupported. The system does not infer a causal meal effect from a note, and it does not simulate the effect of changing a medication without separate validated evidence.

## 17. Practical data intake

The intake screen gives the prototype a practical integration boundary. An anonymized CSV can provide timestamped glucose observations. A synthetic streaming control can send successive native-cadence readings through the same backend path. A limited local FHIR adapter accepts supported patient and clinical resources while checking identity references, required information and units. Observation time and arrival time are explicit. Duplicate inputs are handled idempotently, and invalid references or unsupported timing are rejected. The local adapter is an interoperability demonstration. It is not proof of live ABDM participation or a complete hospital integration. Official vendor CGM access is also a separate milestone. Some integrations can deliver historical or delayed observations, which cannot support a current thirty-minute warning. A future pilot must measure end-to-end latency rather than infer it from a vendor name. In the public demonstration, intake stays within the project's anonymized and clearly synthetic data boundary.

## 18. Consultation summary

The consultation summary is a concrete output a clinician can use to review the record. It contains observed glucose information, the duration and completeness of available data, issued forecasts and their outcomes where known, unresolved review items and documented notes. Gaps and unsuccessful predictions remain visible. The report uses the same evidence as the interface so export does not become a separate source of truth. User-entered notes are handled as text, and protected downloads require the authenticated account. Short or fragmented recordings are identified. A brief recording cannot support the same stable pattern interpretation as a complete longer monitoring period. The summary therefore reports its observation coverage and evidence boundary instead of presenting an unsupported long-term assessment. For the hackathon, exporting this document closes the core journey and gives judges an artifact they can inspect. Future clinical use still requires the agreed validation, data arrangements and operating protocol.

## 19. Access, audit and recovery

Deployment controls make the workflow reviewable and recoverable. The application authenticates users and distinguishes reviewer and administrator responsibilities. Patient routes are protected, and clinic identity limits access to records. Initial local credentials are generated into an ignored runtime location rather than committed to the public source. Secrets come from the environment or protected local runtime files. The database persists review and forecast records, and action history is retained. The backup tool uses SQLite's consistent backup mechanism, which includes committed changes even when write-ahead logging is enabled. Restore is an offline operation and does not silently overwrite an existing database. The local check verifies integrity and that stored review records survive recovery. These controls are engineering evidence. A future deployment must also address encrypted transport and storage, consent and retention, incident response and applicable privacy obligations. Successful authentication is not a certificate of compliance or clinical readiness.

## 20. Clinical pilot gates

The clinical roadmap starts with intended use, responsibility and evidence. The partner doctor should review the five prepared cases, agree staffed hours and response ownership, and identify misleading displays or inappropriate implied actions. That provides usability feedback, not clinical effectiveness. Authorized device timing and identity matching must be verified before live short-horizon warnings. Privacy, regulatory and ethics requirements need assessment for the intended use and pilot design. Prospective shadow evaluation should record frozen-model forecasts without influencing patient care, allowing performance and workload to be measured in the intended setting. Only after the agreed gates pass should a supervised clinical-use stage begin. The Shanghai benchmark does not establish Indian clinic performance. We retain this boundary because the product's credibility depends on describing what exists. The pilot roadmap is actionable, but a timetable alone cannot establish that its evidence or approval requirements have been met.
